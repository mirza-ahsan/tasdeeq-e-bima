The problem

Claims get rejected for paperwork, not medicine

  • A clinic treats an insured patient, then bills the insurer
  • Weeks later the claim bounces: no pre-authorisation, a missed filing deadline, missing documents, an amount over tariff
  • Staff rework and resubmit while the money is stuck
  • Small clinics have no billing specialist to catch it
Pre-authorisation

Insurer approval needed before a planned treatment. Miss it and correct, necessary care can still go unpaid.

94% of US physicians say it delays care.9

Why it matters

Avoidable rejections are a financial-inclusion problem

  • Governments are scaling health insurance to close the coverage gap
  • That means millions of new claims, new rules — and new rejections
  • Clinics that aren’t paid stop accepting insured patients
  • The patients insurance was meant to protect lose access

How it works

How it works

It asks only the questions that matter

  • Picks whichever question tells it the most about this claim
  • Asks about pre-authorisation first when the procedure needs it — never when it doesn’t
  • Updates the chance of rejection after every answer
  • Stops once the verdict is settled: never under 3 questions, never over 8

What the clinic sees

A verdict it can act on

  • Chance of rejection, with a low / medium / high verdict
  • Which field is causing it, in points
  • Six insurer rules: pre-auth, deadline, tariff, coverage, diagnosis fit, patient fit
  • The official rejection code (X12 CARC) and the one thing to fix
  • A plain-English summary

Honest limits

What it does not do

  • Not trained on real claims — no public dataset exists. Records are simulated (Synthea); the rules and rejection codes are real
  • Doesn’t replace staff — it is a second check before submitting
  • Doesn’t submit claims or talk to insurers
  • Doesn’t learn by itself — staff corrections are logged, not auto-applied
  • No login or encryption yet — built for a demo with synthetic data
